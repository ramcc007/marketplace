Job distribution software takes one job advert and publishes it to many job boards, search engines and social channels. It then reports back on where applications came from, so a recruiter can see which boards produced candidates. These tools usually sit beside an applicant tracking system rather than replacing it.

This page compares the six products we found that meet all three must-have capabilities on their own vendor pages: posting to many job boards, posting budget management and applicant source tracking. It does not rank them. Hiresome publishes plan prices. The other five publish no price, so their entries describe what each vendor says its product does.

## Why the three capabilities go together

Posting to many boards is the basic service. Budget management and source tracking turn it into a spend decision. Without a budget control, a team can overspend on boards that bring few hires. Without source tracking, it cannot tell which spend worked. A tool that only posts widely gives reach but no evidence about return.

## The six products

[Broadbean](/products/broadbean) says its platform connects to more than 7,000 job boards, search engines and social media channels. Its product page describes a custom quota on job board credits, which lets a team limit spend. It also says the platform tracks the source of every candidate application, and that its reporting supports decisions at channel level and at team level.

[Appcast](/products/appcast) advertises jobs across what it calls a source-neutral network of thousands of sites. Its homepage says the platform allocates the recruitment marketing budget across every channel. It also says the platform helps teams identify the sources that drive qualified applicants. The homepage gives no count of boards.

[Joveo](/products/joveo) says jobs are posted across hundreds of duration and slot-based job boards. Its job distribution page says teams can centralize and manage media spends across distributed recruiting teams. It also describes tracking media buying campaigns from the source click through to application and hire. Joveo has the weakest budget evidence of the three, because its page describes managing spend across teams rather than setting a posting budget for each job.

[Logicmelon](/products/logicmelon) says a job ad can be written once and sent to a choice of hundreds of free and paid job boards. Its multi-posting page says a team can centralize spend and assign access and credits to team members. It also says the product shows where applications come from.

[JobTarget](/products/jobtarget) says a job added to its Marketplace cart appears on as many sites as the buyer wants. Its FAQ says a team can manage spend and renewals from one dashboard, and that the Marketplace reports which boards produce applicants. Its budget evidence is thinner than Broadbean's, because the page describes dashboard control of spend and credits without a per-posting cap.

[Hiresome](/products/hiresome) says a buyer can stop logging into several job boards to post the same role. Its job boards page says sponsored posts and job board budgets can be managed from one central dashboard. It also says the buyer can track which boards deliver the best candidates and adjust spending. Hiresome is the only product in this group whose pricing page lists plan amounts.

## Budget controls vary in strength

The six pages use different budget language. Broadbean describes a quota, which is a hard limit on credits. Hiresome describes a central dashboard for sponsored posts and board budgets. Logicmelon describes centralized spend and credits. Appcast describes automatic allocation, which moves budget between channels without a stated cap. JobTarget and Joveo describe central management of spend. None of the pages explains how a budget is set, approved or reported against a single posting.

Several other vendors read on one page did not state a budget control or source tracking. Idibu's page describes multiposting and general reporting, but not spend control or source tracking. Recruitly's page describes posting to 50 or more boards and source attribution, but not budget caps. Recruitics describes reaching candidates across channels, but not spend control. Join describes multiposting in its plans but not spend or source analytics. SmartRecruiters' SmartDistribute page did not describe any of the three. Vonq, Pinpoint and Firefish returned errors when we tried to read their pages, so we could not confirm them.

## Pricing you can and cannot see

Hiresome's pricing page lists Lite at $4 and Pro at $16 a month per recruiter, both billed yearly, and an Enterprise plan by quote. The other five publish no price on the pages we read. Broadbean's product page and Appcast's homepage offer only demo requests. Joveo's pricing page describes volume-based discounts per transaction and gives no amount. Logicmelon's multi-posting page shows only a demo link. JobTarget says there are no platform fees for its Marketplace, and that pricing depends on the boards selected and the postings bought, but it shows no amount.

For those five, the profile records the pricing model as quote only, with no amount. Ask each vendor for the price in writing before comparing them, and ask whether job board fees are billed on top of any platform fee.

## Source tracking in practice

Source tracking means each applicant record names the board or campaign that produced it. Broadbean says it tracks the source of every candidate application. Hiresome and Logicmelon each say they show which boards or sources bring candidates. JobTarget says its Marketplace reports which boards produce applicants. Appcast says it links job ad performance to candidate behavior and ATS outcomes. Joveo tracks media campaigns through to hire. Each claim should be tested with a real job before a contract is signed.

## What to check before you choose

Count the boards you need, and check whether each one is in the vendor's network rather than relying on the vendor's total. Ask for a monthly spend cap in writing, and test whether it stops posts when reached. Confirm that the source field reaches your applicant tracking system, because a report in the distribution tool does not help if the ATS drops the field.
