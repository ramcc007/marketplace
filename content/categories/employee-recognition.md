Employee recognition software lets colleagues thank each other in a shared feed, can attach points or rewards to that thanks, and reports on who gets recognized. This page compares eight products: Assembly, Bonusly, Awardco, Nectar, Recognize, Vantage Circle, Achievers and WorkTango. We read each vendor's pricing page and, where a claim needed more support, a small number of its feature pages. Where a page did not show something, we say so rather than fill the gap.

## Why buyers look at these tools

Gallup's engagement indicator says 27% of U.S. employees report receiving recognition or praise for good work in the past seven days, as of August 2026. Gallup's recognition article, first published on June 28, 2016 and updated on January 12, 2024, says employees who do not feel adequately recognized are twice as likely to say they will quit within a year. It recommends recognition every seven days.

The same article reports where the most memorable recognition came from. Managers accounted for 28%, senior leaders or the CEO for 24%, a manager's manager for 12%, customers for 10% and peers for 9%. The remaining 17% was counted as other. A tool built only around peer posts covers the smallest share of that list, so buyers should ask how managers and leaders take part.

This page assumes a buyer who wants recognition that is visible, budgeted and measurable, without buying a full engagement suite. If surveys are the main goal, start with [employee engagement software](/software/employee-engagement) instead.

## What counts as recognition software here

A product is included when its own pages show three things. Any employee can recognize a colleague. The product offers a rewards catalog or points, so recognition can carry a value. The product reports on recognition activity. A product that mentions recognition as one line in a larger suite is left out unless its pages show all three.

Evidence is uneven. Some entries rest on a pricing page alone, and the sections below name the gaps where a vendor did not show a criterion. Suites that cover recognition alongside reviews and surveys are discussed under [performance management](/software/performance-management) and are not compared here.

## Who publishes a price

Three of the eight publish any price: Assembly, Bonusly and Awardco. The other five send you to a quote form or a demo.

[Assembly](/products/assembly) lists its Empower plan at $3 per user per month, billed yearly. Its Enterprise plan has no price, and the page sends you to a demo. The page does not name a currency, so we read the dollar sign as US dollars.

[Bonusly](/products/bonusly) lists Team at $3 per user per month, or $30 per user a year. Team with its Bizy assistant is $5 per user per month, or $50 a year, and the Bizy add-on is shown at $2 per user per month. The Organization plan is quoted on request. The page also contradicts itself on billing. The pricing toggle offers monthly billing, while an FAQ says all plans are billed annually. Ask Bonusly which one applies before you build a budget. The page offers a 30-day trial.

[Awardco](/products/awardco) shows starter packages from $3,000 for businesses with 100 employees or less. The visible text names no billing period and no currency, and the figure is a starting point for flexible packages rather than a fixed plan price. Higher plans show no price, and the plan names differ between the plan cards and the FAQ.

The other five publish no price. [Recognize](/products/recognize) shows a custom price button on each tier. [Vantage Circle](/products/vantage-circle) says its cost depends on organization size, selected products and required integrations. [WorkTango](/products/worktango) says annual cost depends on employee count, and it uses request buttons. [Achievers](/products/achievers) asks for a quote that fits your team. [Nectar](/products/nectar) offers only a pricing request and a demo button.

A rough example shows why the subscription is not the whole bill. At Assembly's list price, 100 people on Empower would pay $300 a month, or $3,600 a year, before any reward budget. Awardco's $3,000 is a starting figure with no billing period shown, so it cannot be turned into an annual total. Neither figure includes the rewards employees redeem.

## The reward budget is a separate cost

Recognition programs have two costs: the software, and the rewards that employees redeem. The vendors handle the reward money differently, so ask before you sign.

Recognize's FAQ says money deposited for rewards is returned to you with a $50 processing fee. It offers a full refund if you ask within the first 30 days, and says there are no refunds after the first month of an annual subscription. The page also says the company charges annually.

WorkTango says its minimum contract is 12 months. Achievers says no annual points commitment is required, and that its rewards carry no shipping charges and no surprise markups. Bonusly offers a 30-day trial.

Before you sign, ask each vendor three things in writing. Get the date when money leaves your account, whether at deposit or at redemption. Get the rule for unspent points at the end of a year. Get the name of the party that holds the money if you cancel.

## Points, catalogs and the tax question

Recognition rewards can be taxable pay. IRS Publication 15-B, the 2026 revision, says the achievement award exclusion covers up to $1,600 a year for qualified plan awards, and up to $400 a year for awards that are not qualified plan awards. Gift cards, gift coupons and gift certificates are not excludable as achievement awards. The one exception is an arrangement that lets the employee choose only tangible property from a limited, preselected list. The same publication says cash equivalents, including gift cards, are never excludable as de minimis benefits.

A catalog that is mostly gift cards may therefore create taxable pay that your payroll team has to handle. Ask each vendor whether it reports reward values to payroll, and ask your tax adviser how your own program should be treated. The pricing pages we read do not describe tax reporting.

The catalogs differ. Nectar's catalog lists gift cards, Amazon, swag and custom rewards. Vantage Circle lists an Amazon store, experiences and holidays, gift vouchers and merchandise, and its page does not mention points. Recognize includes gift card rewards in its Start-Up, Mid-Market and Enterprise columns. WorkTango describes a rewards marketplace with millions of options, and Achievers describes a global rewards marketplace. Neither gives a count of items.

We count Vantage Circle's catalog as evidence for the rewards criterion, but it is the weakest evidence on this page, because the page does not describe points at all. For any catalog, count how many items are physical goods or experiences, and how many are gift cards.

## What the analytics cover

Recognition analytics range from a count of participation to a report on who is being overlooked. The pages we read describe very different depth.

Awardco lists more than 15 standard report templates that export to CSV or send on a schedule through its REST API. It says engagement analytics need a separate product, Awardco Engage, which it sells alone or bundled with Recognize.

Nectar's Recognize feature list includes recognition analytics and engagement reporting. Recognize's comparison table marks people analytics as included in its Start-Up, Mid-Market and Enterprise columns. Vantage Circle places basic analytics and insights on its Grow plan and advanced insights on Transform.

Assembly's Empower plan lists advanced people analytics. That wording covers people data in general, not recognition reports in particular, so ask for a sample. Bonusly's pricing page does not say which plan includes analytics. The Achievers and WorkTango pricing pages we read do not describe analytics at all, so we could not confirm either vendor's reporting from them.

When you compare, ask for a sample report that shows recognition by team and by manager. Gallup's split puts peers at 9% of memorable recognition, so a program where managers give most of the praise will need that gap made visible.

## Contract terms to check

Annual billing is common here. Recognize says it charges annually, Assembly bills Empower yearly, and WorkTango states a 12-month minimum contract. Bonusly's pricing page contradicts itself, as noted above. Achievers and Nectar do not state a term on the pricing pages we read.

Free trials are rare. Bonusly offers 30 days, and Recognize says a free trial is available from the top of its site. Most of the others point you to a quote request or a demo.

On security, the pricing pages we read do not state SOC 2 or ISO 27001 status for any of the eight vendors. That is a gap in what we checked, not proof that no report exists. Ask each vendor for its current SOC 2 report under a non-disclosure agreement, read the audit period, and ask which plan includes single sign-on.

## Integrations

Recognition works best where employees already talk. Nectar lists integrations with Slack, Microsoft Teams, UKG, ADP, Rippling, Paycor, Workday and Okta. Bonusly's home page links to Slack and Microsoft Teams integration pages, but the pages we read do not say whether those integrations cost extra.

Check the people-data feed as well. Recognition budgets, manager alerts and milestone messages depend on correct hire dates, managers and locations. If your records live in an [HRIS](/software/hris), ask how often the vendor syncs. If rewards touch pay, ask how the vendor works with your [payroll](/software/payroll) provider.

## What to test during a trial or demo

- Send a recognition from a test account, then confirm who sees it, who can comment on it and who can delete it.
- Open the catalog as an employee in your smallest country and count the redeemable items that are not gift cards.
- Ask for the budget screen a manager sees, and check whether unspent funds roll over.
- Request a sample report on recognition by team and by manager, and check that it can be exported.
- Get the full price in writing, including reward funding, minimum term, single sign-on and HRIS add-ons, and cancellation terms.
- Ask how the vendor reports reward values for tax, and compare the answer with your payroll provider's process.
