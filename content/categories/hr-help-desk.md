An HR help desk gives employees one place to ask HR questions, open a case and see what happens next. It replaces the shared inbox that many HR teams use for pay, leave and policy questions. Software in this category should answer three questions for an employee: where to open a case, where to find an answer without opening one, and how to see the status of a request already sent.

This page compares eight products that meet all three on their own vendor sites. Each product has evidence on the vendor's domain for employee case intake, an HR knowledge base and case tracking. The figures below come from those pages or from the product profiles on this site. Only one of the eight publishes an HR price, so the price comparison is thin.

## Why the three capabilities belong together

A knowledge base alone does not stop the email. Employees read policy pages, still send a question to HR, and HR still sorts the inbox by hand. A case queue alone sends every routine question to a person, including the ones an article would answer. Case tracking is the part that stops the follow-up message. Without it, an employee asks the same question again because nobody confirmed that the request arrived.

The rule for this page is that each product needs all three capabilities on its own vendor site, in one product. Aggregator and review sites were used to find candidates only. No product is listed on an aggregator figure alone.

The capability must also be about HR cases and HR questions from employees. A generic IT service desk counts only when its own vendor pages describe an HR use for employee cases. Several IT service desks failed that test, as the last section explains.

## The eight products

Workday Help is the knowledge and case module in Workday's Human Capital Management suite. The Workday admin guide says employees use self-service to create cases, check their statuses and connect with the assigned case solver. Employees see suggested articles before they open a case. The product page shows Contact Sales and no prices.

Zendesk Employee Service applies Zendesk's ticketing to HR. The HR page describes one workspace for employee requests, a searchable AI-powered help centre and AI agents for routine HR questions. Zendesk's HR help desk guide says employees can track the status of their HR inquiries. That sentence sits in a general buying section of the guide, so it is the thinnest of the three sources for this product.

LBi HR HelpDesk comes from LBi Software. Its HR HelpDesk page lists an employee self-service portal, case creation by email, an FAQ knowledge base, an embedded wiki and real-time case history. The page says cases are tracked to closure. It does not say whether employees, as opposed to HR staff, can see their own case status.

Moveworks is an AI assistant that employees reach in Slack, Microsoft Teams or the web. Its HR solutions page says the assistant answers HR questions from the customer's knowledge base. Its ServiceNow HR access requirements page lists a Create Case call against the HR case table and a Retrieve Ticket Status call. Moveworks has no case queue of its own. It works with the case system it connects to, so its HR features depend on that system.

Ivanti HR Service Management runs on the Ivanti Neurons Platform. Its portal help page, from the 2023 documentation bundle, lists forms for submitting a new HR case, access to HR knowledge articles and the employee's open HR cases. The product page says managers and employees see status at every step. The help page is older than the product page, so check the current release before relying on it.

Dayforce HR Service Delivery adds HR case management and HR knowledge to Dayforce, formerly Ceridian. The mobile app guide for release R2026.2.1 shows an employee choosing a topic, writing a description and submitting a case. The case then appears under My Cases with a system-assigned case number. The HR Service Delivery page says HR Knowledge Management stores information and policies for easy, yet secure, access. That page is older, and it still describes employee case creation as something that will be available later.

SolarWinds Service Desk and ManageEngine ServiceDesk Plus are already profiled on this site under IT service management. Both vendors also publish HR pages that meet the three criteria. The SolarWinds HR page says employees submit HR requests, such as benefits questions and pay change requests, through a dedicated self-service portal and track them in real time. It also says employees find answers in a searchable knowledge base. The ManageEngine HR page lists request templates for collecting employee issues, a user-specific knowledge base with FAQs, and a self-service portal where employees check ticket status.

## What the prices look like

LBi is the only product here with a published figure. Its news page gives a starting price of $1,530 a year on annual billing. The page does not say which tier that figure covers. The Free tier covers up to 100 employees and two administrative users, with no expiry. Pro covers up to 500 employees and 10 administrative users, and ProPlus covers up to 2,000 employees and 20. Pro and ProPlus carry 30-day free trials. The page shows a dollar sign with no currency code, so the figure is recorded as shown.

Zendesk's pricing page lists a Support Team plan at US$19 per agent a month billed annually. That is Zendesk's customer service plan. It is not Employee Service, and the page shows no Employee Service price. The same page's title reads "Starting from £19/month" while its plan cards show US$ 19, so the currency is unclear there.

The other five products are quote-only on the pages read. Workday Help, Moveworks, Ivanti HR Service Management and Dayforce HR Service Delivery show a demo or sales button and no figures. SolarWinds and ManageEngine show prices, but in their profiles those prices are IT service desk plans priced per technician, so they do not describe the HR module.

Quote-only pricing depends on headcount, modules and the ticketing system a product connects to. Moveworks is the clearest case, because its HR case features depend on a system such as ServiceNow, and the page we read does not price that system.

## Limits and terms

Most products on this list set terms by employee count, administrator count or module. LBi's tiers set limits by employees and administrators, so headcount decides the tier. Count every person who can raise a case, not only the active staff. Zendesk's HR page offers a 14-day free trial.

Dates matter for two products. Dayforce's HR Service Delivery page describes employee case creation as something still to come, while its newer mobile guide shows employees creating cases. Ivanti's portal help page is from the 2023 bundle. Feature names and availability change between releases, so confirm the release that includes employee case creation and status.

None of the pages read for this draft describe data retention, export formats or the terms for ending a contract. Ask each vendor for those in writing before you sign.

## Products checked and left out

Atlassian Jira Service Management has an HR template where employees submit requests and reach a self-service knowledge base. Its HR pages do not say employees can track their own HR requests. The request status view in its support documentation is a general portal feature, not an HR statement, so the product is left out.

ServiceNow HR Service Delivery could not be checked. Its product pages returned HTTP 403 Forbidden, and the one community article that loaded did not describe employee knowledge or status.

Oracle HR Help Desk could not be checked. Its product page returned HTTP 403 Forbidden, and the HCM documentation page that loaded had no readable body text.

UKG Pro People Assist could not be checked. Its product pages returned HTTP 403 Forbidden.

iTouchVision HR Helpdesk could not be verified. Its HR help desk page loaded only the title. Listing sites describe the product, but their figures are not used here.

SysAid's HR solution page redirected to an IT page, so its HR claims could not be read. Its documentation shows employees submitting requests and checking their status, but it does not name HR cases.

HappyFox's HR page describes HR forms and a knowledge base. It says employees can track approvals and find policies, but it does not say they can track their HR cases.

Kissflow describes an HR help desk with self-service answers. Its page says HR sees the queue, but it does not say employees can view the status of their questions.

Freshservice's HR material describes self-service knowledge bases and staff status updates. It does not describe employee-visible status.

Alloy Navigator's HR portal includes a service catalog, a knowledge base and an issue form. Its page does not describe status tracking for employees.

HR Cloud's onboarding page does not describe an HR help desk, employee case intake or case tracking. HiBob and BambooHR offer help centres and request views, but we found no employee case queue with status. HR Acuity is an employee relations tool for investigations, and we did not find a verified HR knowledge base on its pages.

Workday HCM, the HRIS product, is listed under the HRIS category. Its case features sit in Workday Help, which is listed above.

## Testing before you pay

Pick one real HR question and one real case from the last quarter. Ask each vendor to show both in a demo or trial. Check three things: whether an employee can open a case from email, the portal and mobile, whether the employee can see the case status without asking HR, and whether a relevant knowledge article appears before the case is created.

Count the people who can raise a case, then compare that count with the tier limits. For LBi, the employee count sets the tier.

For Moveworks, ask which system holds the HR cases and whether the assistant reads the same status that employees see. For Dayforce and Ivanti, confirm the release that includes employee case creation and status before you rely on the pages read here.

Ask each vendor for data retention, export format and the terms for leaving in writing. Ask for the currency on any quote before you sign.
