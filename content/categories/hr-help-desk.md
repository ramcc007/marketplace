An HR help desk gives employees one place to ask HR questions, open a case and see what happens next. It replaces the shared inbox that many HR teams use for pay, leave and policy questions. Software in this category should answer three questions for an employee: where to open a case, where to find an answer without opening one, and how to see the status of a request already sent.

One product met all three criteria on its vendor's own pages: LBi HR HelpDesk. Five others were read and left out because one of the three capabilities was missing, implied, or stated only on a how-to or administrator page. The list is short because the test is strict.

## Why the three capabilities belong together

A knowledge base alone does not stop the email. Employees read policy pages, still send a question to HR, and HR still sorts the inbox by hand. A case queue alone sends every routine question to a person, including the ones an article would answer. Case tracking stops the follow-up message, because an employee can see that a request arrived and what is happening to it.

## The product that meets all three

LBi's HR HelpDesk page describes email-to-case creation, a full knowledge base in FAQs, and real-time case history. The case history is stated on the vendor's page, but the page does not say clearly what an employee sees in it, so the status view is the first thing to test in a demo.

## Products checked and left out

Workday Help's administrator guide describes case management, and its knowledge page describes knowledge articles and cases. The intake and tracking claims rest on the administrator guide rather than a product page, so they are not counted.

Zendesk Employee Service's HR page describes a searchable help centre. Intake and tracking are implied on its pages rather than stated, so neither is counted.

Moveworks' documentation covers access requirements for HR cases in ServiceNow, and its HR page describes searching a customer's own knowledge bases. Neither shows the product's own case intake or its own knowledge base.

Ivanti HR Service Management's portal document is from 2023 and describes case intake and knowledge only in general terms. Its product page does state that managers and employees see status at every step, so tracking is confirmed but the other two are not.

Dayforce HR Service Delivery's cited pages are a mobile how-to and a blog article. Neither names the product, so it is not counted.

## What the pricing looks like

This page does not compare prices. The profile for the qualifying product records the price terms its vendor page shows. Check those terms against the vendor page before relying on them, and ask every vendor for a quote on the same number of employees and cases before you compare.

## Testing before you pay

Open three test cases as different employees: a pay question, a leave question, and a question that an article should answer. Check that the article is offered before a case opens, that each case has an owner and a status, and that the employee can see the status without writing to HR. Ask how cases created from email are logged, and whether a deleted case stays in the history.

Look at who answers each case as well. A help desk that routes a pay question to a generic queue will frustrate employees even when the software works. Ask for a demo of routing rules for a sensitive case, such as a complaint or a leave request that needs confidentiality, and check whether the people who see the case can be limited. Reporting matters too. Ask which fields the tool records for each case, how long cases stay open on average, and whether an export keeps the full history. A help desk that cannot show volume by topic will not show HR where its policies confuse people.
