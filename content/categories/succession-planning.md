Succession planning software keeps a record of who could fill a critical role and how ready each person is. Tools in this group usually hold a pool of potential successors, rate how ready each one is, and support a talent review where managers and HR compare people across teams. Each of the three terms describes a step in a process, so a product has to show every step rather than a general talent feature.

This page sets out what each must-have means and reports which vendors we checked. One product, Oracle Fusion Cloud Talent Management, meets all three on pages that Oracle publishes. The category therefore lists one product. The category needs eight to be indexed, and this page does not pad the list with weaker matches.

## What the three capabilities mean

A successor pool is a group of named people who could move into a role. Pools are often built by role family rather than one name per position. A readiness assessment rates each person in the pool on how soon they could take a role, such as ready now, ready in one to two years, or development needed. A talent review is a scheduled meeting where managers and HR compare people on performance and potential, often using a nine-box grid.

These three are separate. A tool can show a nine-box grid without holding any successors, and a tool can hold a list of successors without rating readiness. The must-have test checks for all three together, which is stricter than many talent tools need to be.

## The product that meets the test

Oracle's help page on succession plans, talent pools and talent reviews says a user can select members from talent pools they own and add them as candidates to succession plans they manage. That covers successor pools. The same page says succession plans and talent pools can be associated with a talent review meeting, and that members of a talent pool can be rated during the meeting. That covers talent reviews.

Readiness is the step that most tools leave out. Oracle's 26B release notes for the Team Succession Planning Dashboard list the names of successor candidates with a readiness of Ready Now, and the names of team members who have no Ready-Now successor. Those notes name Ready Now as the only readiness level shown, and they do not say who sets it. A buyer should ask Oracle to show how a readiness level is set and recorded for each candidate before relying on it.

Oracle's overview page says the module needs an HCM Base or Talent Management Base to be implemented first. The pages we read publish no price, so the listing is quote-only and states no amount.

## Vendors checked and left out

Fuel50 launched a succession module in October 2025. Its page describes pipelines built by role and by skill, and a Skills Matrix with three levels: ready now, ready soon and emergency coverage. The page does not say how those levels are set. It criticises once-a-year reviews and nine-box style grids, and it does not describe a talent review meeting. The page publishes no prices and sends buyers to sales. Fuel50 does not meet the test on the pages read.

SAP SuccessFactors is the strongest candidate on talent pools. SAP's own learning pages say managers nominate employees for talent pools or as successors for key positions, and that nominations are confirmed in a talent review meeting. SAP's knowledge base says talent pools let users group successors. The pages we could open did not describe a readiness level for successors. SAP's product pages returned an access error, so the feature scope document could not be read. SAP is not counted until its own pages show readiness for successors.

Cornerstone's platform and overview pages describe ready successors and an AI assistant that surfaces bench strength. Those pages do not state successor pools, a readiness rating or a talent review meeting. Cornerstone's articles describe talent reviews and nine-box grids in general terms. Articles about practice are not product features, so they were not counted. Cornerstone's pricing page shows no amounts and links to a package page.

Head-Light's Talent Successor page describes successor pools and a line about continuously tracking succession readiness. It does not give a readiness scale for successors, and it does not describe a talent review or a nine-box grid. The page shows no price and asks for a contact form.

Engagedly, Peoplebox, PeopleFluent, Eightfold, Leapsome and Gloat did not state all three terms on the pages read in the first pass. UKG's homepage returned an error. Quantum Workplace's succession page returned a not found error, so it was not counted.

## Pricing is quote-based where we could see it

No product in this category has a published price on the pages we read. The Oracle pages read show no price for the module. Fuel50's page publishes none. Cornerstone's succession page and Head-Light's page show no amounts either. Peoplebox's FAQ says pricing depends on employee count and the modules chosen, and it directs buyers to a custom quote. Packages can include some features and not others, so a buyer should ask in writing for successor pools, readiness ratings and talent reviews to be part of the quote.

## What a buyer should test

Ask for a demonstration of a named successor pool for one critical role. Check that a readiness rating can be set for each successor, with a date and an owner. Then run a talent review with two managers and see whether the output feeds back into the successor pool. A product that cannot do this in a demonstration does not meet the test, whatever its marketing says.
