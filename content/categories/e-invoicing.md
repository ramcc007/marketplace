E-invoicing software turns an invoice into structured data that a tax authority or a customer's system can read without retyping. It then delivers that data over an approved route and keeps a copy for the legal retention period. A PDF attached to an email does not do this on its own. This page covers the products in this category and the rules that make the question urgent.

This draft has verified published prices and archive terms for two products, Qvalia and Invopop. Six other products are listed for review and have no figures here. For basic invoice creation and payment links, see [invoicing software](/software/invoicing).

## What a compliant invoice needs

Three things decide whether a product fits. The first is a government-accepted format. A format is a defined data layout, not a visual document, and countries name their own. Examples include XRechnung and ZUGFeRD in Germany, Facturae in Spain, SDI in Italy and KSeF in Poland. Some countries only accept the layout. Others require the invoice to pass through a tax authority platform before the customer receives it.

The second is delivery. An invoice can travel through an agreed network, or through a government platform, instead of ordinary email. Products that support several countries need one connection per route, so ask which route each country uses.

The third is archiving. Many countries require the structured original to be kept for years, in a form that shows it has not changed. A product that only lets you download a PDF may not meet that test. This draft counts a product as having archive terms only where its own pages describe storage.

## Why the rules are moving

The European Union's [ViDA package](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-digital-age-vida_en) entered into force on 14 April 2025. Digital reporting requirements for cross-border B2B transactions start on 1 July 2030. Member states that already run domestic real-time reporting must align their systems with the EU model by 1 January 2035.

National deadlines come sooner. Since 1 January 2026, [Belgian VAT-liable enterprises](https://einvoice.belgium.be/en) have had to use structured electronic invoices between businesses. In Poland, [KSeF](https://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/informacje-ogolne-ksef-20/zakres-obowiazkowego-ksef/) became mandatory for issuing invoices on 1 February 2026 for taxpayers whose 2024 sales exceeded PLN 200 million. For other taxpayers the date is 1 April 2026. Receiving invoices through KSeF has been mandatory for all taxpayers since 1 February 2026.

The practical effect is that a business selling into several countries faces several rules at once. A product that handles one route well may not handle another. Ask each vendor for the list of countries it covers today, with the date each one went live.

## Two products with published prices

Qvalia prices by message. Its Connect Small plan costs €9 a month for 25 messages, Connect Medium costs €39 for 100 and Connect Large costs €99 for 1,000. The 1X, 2X and 3X plans cost €299, €499 and €899 a month for 5,000, 12,000 and 30,000 messages. Extra messages cost €1 each on Small and Medium and €0.50 each on Large.

Archiving is where Qvalia's plans split. Its pricing page marks archiving as not included on Small and Medium, and included on Large and above. A business that needs only a few archived invoices a month therefore has to buy Large at €99 a month to get the archive. Ask what happens to stored invoices if you move to a smaller plan.

Invopop is built for software companies that add e-invoicing to their own product. Its Dev plan is free and includes 200 Pops, which are its usage credits, and one government app. Pro costs €500 a month and also includes 200 Pops and one government app. Each additional government app costs €300 a month. Sending one SDI invoice to Italy uses 5 Pops in its workflow, which makes the cost of a busy Italian month easy to estimate.

## Archive terms

Retention periods differ by country, and a single vendor statement is not enough. Ask what period applies in each country you sell into, and whether the product keeps the structured original or only a PDF rendering. Ask also what it costs to keep access after you cancel. An archive you cannot open after the contract ends is not an archive.

## Security and contracts

This draft did not collect security certification statements for the products on the page, so no security field is recorded. Ask each vendor which reports cover the specific e-invoicing service you would use, and whether they apply to the archive as well as the messaging.

## What to test during a trial

Send a test invoice to a customer in your largest market and confirm it arrives in the format that country requires. Receive a test invoice from a supplier over the network and check that line items, tax and totals match the original. Ask the vendor to show where an archived invoice is stored and how you export it.

Count the messages in a typical month and compare the total with the plan allowance and the overage price. Request the country list and the live date for each country in writing before you sign. Ask whether the archive is included on the plan you are buying, since on Qvalia it is included only from the Large plan up.
