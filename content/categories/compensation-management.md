Compensation management software runs the yearly pay decisions. It sets salary bands, allocates merit and bonus budgets to managers, calculates the increases and checks whether pay gaps are widening before any change reaches an employee. This page compares the products that our vendor-page review found to cover three capabilities in one product: salary bands, merit and bonus cycles, and pay equity analysis.

Three products meet all three criteria in this first draft. The other candidates either lacked one of the capabilities on the vendor pages we could read, or their vendor pages could not be read at all during this review.

## Why the three capabilities belong together

A merit cycle needs bands to show whether a proposed raise moves someone out of range. A bonus cycle uses the same pay data to set targets and pools. A pay equity check is most useful while a cycle is still open, because it shows which increases would widen a gap before they are approved. A tool that covers only one of these leaves the other two in a spreadsheet.

The criteria apply to one product at a time. A product counts only when its own vendor's site shows all three capabilities. Directory listings, review sites and comparison articles were used to find candidates, but not as evidence. Salary.com is an example of why this matters. Its CompAnalyst product covers bands and pay equity but not bonus planning, and its CompXL product covers merit and bonus but not band management. Two products that each cover part of the set are not combined here.

## The three products

Aeqium's homepage lists salary bands with compa-ratios and equity gaps under a section headed "Compensation Benchmarks, Salary Bands, and Analytics". The same page says the product can "Build merit matrices, bonus formulas, approval chains, and proration logic exactly the way your organization works". It also says the product can "Monitor pay equity, spot retention risks, and understand compensation patterns before they become problems".

Compport's pay equity page says the product helps teams "Design, maintain and visualize compensation bands aligned to your pay practices". The same page asks "What is the pay equity gap?" and says teams can build dashboards to track the metrics that matter. Its compensation planning page lists a Merit Cycles module and a Bonus Planning module, plus sales and long-term incentive plans.

Dayforce's compensation page says organizations can "Award merit, bonus, and equity to eligible employees based on pre-defined rules". It describes pay equity graphs for spotting a gender pay gap, and its FAQ lists pay-equity analysis and guardrails. Its support for salary bands is the thinnest of the three. The page refers to pay ranges and flags for increases that fall out of range, and it quotes a customer who compares pay with the salary band for each job. We did not find a band-setup page on the vendor site, so band configuration needs to be seen in a demo.

## What the prices look like

None of the three publishes a price in the pages we read. Aeqium's plans page lists modules and add-ons without any figure. It says the company will configure a buyer's compensation process at no charge when the buyer requests a demo.

Compport's page says pricing depends on organization size, on the products a buyer chooses, or on a custom package. Dayforce's compensation page shows no price and offers a demo or a sales call. It does not say what a quote covers.

Because no vendor published a figure, this page shows no prices. We did not convert any currency.

## Limits and terms

Aeqium's plans page states no limit on users, employees, budgets or cycles, and it says every plan includes unlimited integrations and unlimited customization. Pay Equity is listed as an add-on under Compensation Analysis, so it is not part of the core plan. The core plan includes compensation bands and cycle management.

The Compport pages we read do not state seat counts, cycle limits or contract length. The Dayforce page does not state a minimum term or contract length either.

Every capability on this page rests on vendor marketing text. A page that says a product sets bands does not show how deep the setup goes, and a page that says a product flags pay gaps does not show how the analysis is calculated. A demo is the only way to see the workflow, and the pages read here cannot replace it.

## Products checked and left out

Payscale has a compensation planning page that covers salary range management, merit cycles and pay equity analysis. That page does not mention bonus cycles. A separate product page says Paycycle runs "merit, bonus, and pay-increase cycles", and its FAQ says ranges are set in Ascent, which is a different product. No single product page showed all three capabilities, so Payscale is left out.

Salary.com sells two products that each cover part of the set, as described above. Neither covers all three in one product.

Figures has clear product pages for salary bands and pay equity, and its compensation review page lists merit increases. We did not find a bonus planning page on its vendor site, so bonus cycles are not confirmed.

Paycom's Compensation Budgeting page describes a merit matrix, one-off bonuses, and salary grades with minimum and maximum reports. Its pay equity text says the tool helps clients comply with pay equity and transparency laws. That describes compliance support rather than an analysis feature, so Paycom is left out on pay equity.

CompLogix names merit, bonus and total rewards in one platform. Its homepage shows no salary band feature and no pay equity analysis.

Carta's pages returned an access error during this review. Search results describe paybands and bonus plans, but we did not confirm merit cycles or a pay equity analysis from Carta's own pages.

Ravio's pages returned rate-limit errors during this review. Search results describe salary bands and pay equity reporting, but we did not find a Ravio page for bonus cycles.

HiBob's English compensation pages show pay bands and bonus allocation. We did not find merit cycles or a pay equity analysis on the English pages we read.

ClayHR's compensation page describes salary bands by role and location, but it does not mention merit cycles or pay equity analysis.

ADP's compensation material describes merit and bonus configuration and a Pay Equity Explorer, but the fact sheet URL returned a not-found error and we found no ADP-owned page for salary bands.

HRSoft's homepage describes merit, bonus and a pay equity analysis module. Salary bands were not confirmed for HRSoft.

Zoho People's compensation page mentions bonuses, but it shows no salary bands, merit cycles or pay equity analysis.

Compease's vendor page redirected to a host that returned an access error, and its bonus and merit features appear only in third-party listings. SAP SuccessFactors and UKG Pro vendor pages returned access errors during this review, so no finding is made for either.

Lattice, Deel, Rippling and Workday already have profiles in this directory, and their vendor pages describe parts of the three capabilities. They are not counted on this draft page. Workday's merit and bonus matrices sit in a course for its Advanced Compensation module, and the product boundary was not confirmed.

## Testing before you pay

Ask each vendor for a demo that uses your own band structure. Load a few roles with their ranges, run one merit cycle with a bonus pool, and check whether an out-of-range proposal is flagged. Then run the pay equity report on a copy of your pay data and see which groups it shows. A vendor may sell the analysis features separately, and the pages read here do not say how they are priced, so ask for a quote that names every module and add-on. Write down the quote basis and the currency. Finally, confirm that all three features work in one account, because a demo that shows each one in a separate session does not prove that the product does all three.
