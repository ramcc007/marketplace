Compensation management software runs the pay decisions that happen once or twice a year. It holds salary bands, allocates merit and bonus budgets to managers, works out each increase and checks whether a change widens a pay gap before it reaches an employee. This page covers the products that our review of vendor pages found doing all three jobs in one product.

One product met all three criteria on its vendor's own pages: Aeqium. Other products were read and left out because one of the three capabilities was missing or only mentioned in passing. The list is short because the test is strict, not because the category is empty.

## Why the three capabilities belong together

A merit cycle needs bands to show whether a proposed raise moves someone out of range. A bonus cycle uses the same pay data to set targets and pools. A pay equity check is most useful while a cycle is still open, because it can show which increases would widen a gap before they are approved. A tool that covers only one of these leaves the other two in a spreadsheet.

The test applies to one product at a time. Salary.com shows why. Its CompAnalyst pages describe pay ranges and pay equity but not bonus planning, while its CompXL pages describe merit and bonus but not band management. Each product covers part of the set, so neither is listed.

## The product that meets all three

Aeqium's homepage lists salary bands, merit matrices with bonus formulas, and pay equity monitoring. Each claim is a sentence on the vendor's own site, and the profile cites that page for all three. The site does not show a price on the pages we read. The pricing page we tried returned a not-found error, so the profile records a quote-only basis and publishes no figure.

## Products checked and left out

Beqom describes merit and bonus cycles and has a separate pay equity product on its own pages. Its solutions page describes pay scales rather than salary bands, so it does not meet the first criterion on the pages we read.

Compport describes salary bands, merit and bonus cycles. Its pay equity page lists gap questions and dashboards but does not describe an analysis, so pay equity is treated as unconfirmed.

Dayforce's compensation page mentions merit, bonus and pay equity graphs. We found no band claim on that page, so it is left out.

Workday Compensation's bands and bonus claims appear only in passing on its product page. Payscale splits band management and merit planning across pages that do not show all three in one product. Pave describes salary ranges and merit cycles but no pay equity analysis. Syndio describes pay equity but no salary bands. Workleap describes bands and merit but no pay equity analysis.

Carta's product page could not be read because the server refused the request, and the Payfactors page returned only its title with no product text. Neither is counted.

## What the pricing looks like

None of the vendor pages we read gives a price for the product that meets the criteria. This page therefore compares what each vendor says it does and how it asks to be priced. It does not compare costs. A buyer should ask for a quote that names each module, the number of employees and the cycle support, in writing.

## Testing before you pay

Ask for a live run of one cycle with your own salary data. Check that the tool sets a band for each job, shows where each proposed raise sits in its band, and flags a pay gap before the approval step. Ask whether bonus pools use the same bands and whether the pay equity report can be run at the same point in the cycle. If any one of these needs a separate module, count it as an extra cost and as a second system to maintain.
