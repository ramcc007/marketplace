Employee wellness software gives staff a shared set of health activities and content, and it reports back to the employer on who takes part. Most products in this group sell challenges, such as step counts or mindfulness weeks, alongside health screening, coaching or a content library. The reporting is what lets an employer show that the program was used, so it matters as much as the activity itself.

This page compares the seven products that meet all three must-have capabilities on their vendor pages: wellness challenges, health programs or content, and participation reports. It does not rank them. It also does not say which product improves health, because the pages we read made no outcome claims we could check. Wellable, Vantage Fit and Incentfit publish prices. Healthper, Wellnify, CoreHealth and Reward Gateway publish none, so their entries describe what the vendor says the product does.

## What the three capabilities mean

A wellness challenge is a time-limited goal that employees join, such as a four-week step challenge. Health programs or content cover the material staff use between challenges, such as a health screening, coaching, mindfulness sessions or a content library. Participation reports show who joined, how often and by which team. A product with challenges but no participation report can run a campaign, but it cannot show an employer who took part.

## The seven products

[Wellable](/products/wellable) runs wellness challenges from a library of more than 40 turnkey challenges. Its challenges page names several, including Move More, Mental Health Matters and Nutrition Ambition. The page says real-time insights give clear visibility into participation and results. Employees track progress in a mobile app or through wearable and fitness app integrations.

[Vantage Fit](/products/vantage-fit) runs step, multi-activity, mindfulness and custom challenges for teams, regions or the whole organization. Its homepage lists a baseline health screening with aggregate insights, guided mindfulness and structured plans with their content. It also says participation, challenge and activity analytics can be read by a board.

[Healthper](/products/healthper) says it offers more than 200 team and individual challenges, including a 12-week mental well-being challenge. Its homepage lists a health assessment, digital and live health coaching and a health library. It also says its reporting covers participation, utilization and risk, through a centralized dashboard.

[Incentfit](/products/incentfit) says it offers unlimited, customizable individual or team challenges on its higher tiers. It gives employees health education, a health encyclopedia and a newsfeed. Its page says the platform delivers real-time dashboards and exportable reports on participation. It does not describe health coaching or screenings.

[Wellnify](/products/wellnify) says organizations can launch challenges and deliver content without custom development. Clients can use Wellnify's evidence-informed content, their own content, or both. Its analytics evidence is the weakest of the seven, because it rests on a dashboard listing and an image description, so a sample report should be checked before buying.

[CoreHealth](/products/corehealth) offers a library of challenges covering physical, nutritional and financial themes, in individual, buddy or team formats. Its page describes 1:1 and group coaching, customizable health assessments and self-paced programs. Its dashboard is described as covering participation metrics, but the page does not say whether reports name individual participants.

[Reward Gateway Corporate Wellbeing](/products/reward-gateway-corporate-wellbeing) runs challenges based on any activity, including walking challenges and habit streaks. It gives access to a library of wellbeing content, including workouts, meditations and recipes. Its page says buyers can download reports on individual performance and engagement. The page does not describe coaching or screenings.

## What each one costs

Wellable's pricing page lists Essentials and Pro at $625 a month each, with $1.25 per user shown beside each price. Enterprise needs a custom quote. The FAQ says the first invoice includes a one-time implementation fee, with no amount given, and that the standard term is one year.

Vantage Fit prices only 200 employees on its pricing page. A Single Challenge costs 1,600 USD on Basic and 2,000 USD on Pro, for one 4-week challenge. Unlimited Challenges cost 3,200 USD on Basic and 4,000 USD on Pro each year. The page says unlimited challenges cost the same as two single challenges. Wellable bills monthly and Vantage Fit bills per challenge or per year, so compare the two at the same headcount before deciding.

Incentfit's pricing page lists per-employee monthly rates in dollars: Challenges at $2.20, Essentials at $3.00, Engage at $4.50 and Empower at $6.00. It also lists $300 a month for dedicated account management, without naming the product it covers.

Healthper, Wellnify, CoreHealth and Reward Gateway show no price on the pages we read. Healthper's program buttons link to Microsoft AppSource, which we did not read. Ask each of these four for a written quote that states the headcount, the term and whether challenges are billed separately.

## Reporting is the test

Ask each vendor for a sample participation report before signing. Check whether it shows participation by team, whether it shows who has taken part in a challenge, and whether it can be exported. A report that shows totals only does not meet the participation report criterion. A report that names individuals raises a privacy question the employer should settle first.

## What to check before you choose

Confirm which challenges are included in the price, because a single challenge and an annual plan are priced differently. Ask whether health content is included or sold separately. Ask how the vendor handles health data, and whether any screening results reach the employer in identifiable form.
