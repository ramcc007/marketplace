A mileage tracking app records business drives, applies a per-mile rate and produces a report that someone can reimburse or file. Some companies pay employees a cents-per-mile rate for the miles they drive for work. Others run an expense system that handles mileage next to receipts. Either way, the app has to capture trips, price them and produce a report.

This page compares seven products on the features and prices their vendor pages publish. Each had to show three things on its own pages: a way to log trips, a rate applied to miles, and a report a manager or accountant can use for reimbursement. The list is one product short of the eight the category needs. The page says so rather than adding an app that fails a check.

## Three checks that decide the list

The first check is trip logging. TripLog's Basic plan logs trips automatically with GPS and has no trip cap. Driversnote logs trips automatically as a team drives, and it flags manual trips that a driver adds. MileIQ tracks mileage automatically, so a driver does not have to stop and log a drive. Timeero captures trips automatically while employees are on the clock, and it records each stop's timestamp and route. Motus's driver app captures GPS-verified mileage. Zoho Expense has the user tap start and stop, or enter an odometer reading. Expensify accepts GPS, start and stop addresses on a map, an odometer photo, or a typed distance.

The second check is the rate. Zoho Expense calculates mileage from pre-set rates, and its help page says the admin sets the vehicle's mileage rate. Expensify lets admins set custom rates by employee group, role or project, or use the IRS mileage rate. MileIQ's Teams Pro plan includes custom rates, and its page does not say whether IRS rates apply. Timeero applies built-in IRS rate calculations. Motus's CPM program pays a per-mile rate that the employer sets, usually the IRS standard rate, and its FAVR program uses IRS-substantiated logic. Driversnote says companies set their own rates, but its page does not say that IRS rates are applied automatically. TripLog lists custom mileage rates in a comparison table without showing which plan includes them.

The third check is the report. Expensify routes mileage into an expense report and says mileage can be exported for tax season. Zoho adds mileage to a report through its Add to Report option. Driversnote managers approve or reject reports from one dashboard, and reports export to PDF or Excel. MileIQ's Teams tier lets drivers submit and review reports, and Teams Pro adds approval and reminders with automatic reporting. Timeero exports detailed mileage reports to payroll software. Motus lists built-in reporting across its driver population and end-to-end reimbursement processing. TripLog lists tax-compliant reports on its pricing page.

## The seven products

Expensify and Zoho Expense are expense platforms. Mileage sits beside receipts and card spend, and their profiles existed before this draft. TripLog, Driversnote, MileIQ and Timeero are mileage apps. Motus runs reimbursement programs for companies with many drivers, and it sells every product by quote. Each product has a profile page in this directory.

The difference matters for the buyer. An expense platform covers receipts, approvals and reimbursement in one place, so a company that already pays for one may not need a separate mileage app. A mileage app covers trips only, and a company still needs a place to pay and approve the claims.

## What the prices look like

Driversnote's free plan covers 15 trips a month. Its Pro plan costs $11 a month per individual for unlimited trips. Its Teams plan costs $11 per license a month for teams of 2 to 10 people. Teams+ is by quote. The iBeacon costs $40 on the free plan and comes free with a yearly subscription on Pro and Teams.

TripLog's Basic plan is free forever, and Premium costs $4.99 a month or $59.99 billed annually. Its pricing page does not name a currency, although the site offers USD, CAD, GBP, EUR and AUD. The page shows single-user prices only.

MileIQ's Teams Lite costs $8 a user a month on monthly billing, Teams costs $9 and Teams Pro costs $10. On annual billing the same plans are $80, $90 and $100 a user a year. The business pricing page shows dollar signs without naming a currency, so the figures are recorded as shown.

Timeero's Basic plan costs $5 a user a month on annual billing and $6 on monthly billing. Pro costs $7.50 and $9, and Premium costs $10 and $12. Enterprise is by contact sales, and the page shows no figure for it.

Motus publishes no prices. Its Motus Reimburse programs are priced per driver by quote, with volume discounts, and contracts are typically annual. Its add-ons are by quote too.

Zoho Expense's free plan covers up to 3 users. Its Premium plan costs $5 per user a month on annual billing, and Premium adds automatic mileage capture. Expensify's Collect plan costs $5 per member a month with no annual commitment, for accounts created on or after April 1, 2025. Its Control plan costs between $9 and $36 an active member, depending on term and card use. Collect gets only a few workspace rules, such as required fields.

## Which kind of product fits

A mileage app prices per individual, per license or per tier, and it covers trips and reports for that individual. A company that pays for a mileage app still needs a second tool for claims and payments. An expense platform prices per member or per user across all expenses, so the mileage cost is part of a larger subscription.

A sales team that drives its own car and needs a simple monthly report is a fit for Driversnote Teams, TripLog Premium or MileIQ Teams Pro. A finance team that already runs receipts and approvals through a platform is a better fit for Expensify or Zoho Expense. A company that pays drivers a fixed per-mile rate across a large fleet is a fit for Motus, and a company that already runs payroll exports on a time-tracking base may find Timeero closer to its process.

## Products left out

Everlance's home page describes automatic GPS detection and team reimbursement, but it does not say whether reimbursements use the IRS rate or a custom rate. Its pricing page also shows two different sets of prices, so the figures are not confirmed here. Hurdlr's pricing page shows no prices, and its page describes automatic tracking without a rate calculation or a reimbursement report. MileageWise's pages describe IRS-proof logs and exports, but no vendor page we read showed a price or a rate calculation, so it is left out.

SAP Concur Drive is GPS-based and sends trips to Concur Expense, but its pricing page returned a 404 error and no public price was found. Fyle's domain could not be reached in the first draft and has not been checked since.

## Testing before you pay

Log five real drives on the free tier, then check the report line by line. Confirm that the rate on the report matches the rate your policy uses, and check whether a manager can change it before approval. Driversnote's free plan stops at 15 trips a month, so a busy driver will hit that limit quickly.

Ask each vendor whether the IRS rate updates on its own, and who maintains it. Ask whether manual trips appear in the report with the same fields as automatic ones, because a report with missing purposes or odometer readings may not satisfy a tax reviewer. For Motus and other quote-only products, ask for the price per driver in writing, along with the contract term and any minimum. Check the export format your accountant needs before you commit to a plan.
