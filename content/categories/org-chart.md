Org chart software draws a company's reporting structure from its people data, and it lets managers test changes before they make them. Good tools pull reporting lines and job titles from an HR system, show every employee in a directory, and let planners model a reorganization without changing the live chart. This page explains what we checked. None of the tools checked so far meets all three criteria, so the category does not yet list a qualifying product.

## What a qualifying tool must show

A product qualifies when its vendor pages show an org chart built from HR data and an employee directory. Both are required. The third criterion is scenario planning or export, and either one is enough. A tool that draws a chart from a spreadsheet only, with no link to HR data, does not qualify, and neither does a tool with a directory but no way to model or export the chart.

A second tool says it keeps org data live and lets leaders play out what-if scenarios. Its page does not name specific HR systems, and the text read did not describe an employee directory or an export feature. It does not qualify yet.

## Why the directory test matters

An org chart without a directory answers only one question, where a person sits in the structure. Managers also need to find a person, see a role's contact details and check who reports to whom in a search. A directory is the feature that turns a chart into a lookup tool. For that reason it is the criterion that separates the tools checked so far.

Scenario planning and export matter for different reasons. Planning lets a leader test a reorganization against the current structure before anything changes in the HR system. Export lets an HR team share a chart with people who have no login. Either one is enough under the criteria, but a tool that offers neither is hard to use for planning.

## Before you choose a tool

Ask each vendor to show a directory view and an export of a sample chart before you buy. Check whether your HR system has a connector, and whether the connector syncs on a schedule or only on demand. A chart that updates once a month can be out of date within days of a hire or a departure. Ask for the currency and the contract length on any quote, and check whether scenario changes stay private until someone publishes them.
