A contractor management tool handles the paperwork and payments for people who work for you without being on your payroll. In practice that means three jobs: getting a contract and onboarding details signed, approving and paying the contractor's invoices, and keeping the tax and compliance records that show the checks were done. This page covers the products that our review of vendor pages found doing all three in one product.

Two products met all three criteria on their own vendor sites: Pebl and Ruul. Six others were read and left out. The list is short because the test needs each capability stated on the vendor's own product pages, not in a guide or an aggregator review.

## Why contractor paperwork needs its own tool

Payroll software is built around employees. Contractors need a contract that describes the work and the payment terms, then the tax and identity records the business keeps for each contractor, and an invoice trail for each payment. Those records sit in different places when a company uses a payroll tool for employees and a spreadsheet for contractors. That gap is what this category is meant to close.

The test applies to one product at a time. A vendor may cover contractor payments in one product and contracts in another. That does not count unless the same product page shows all three capabilities.

## The two products that meet all three

Pebl's contractor management page describes starting onboarding from a single email, generating invoices for rapid payment, and producing 1099-NEC forms. The 1099 point limits the compliance claim to US companies, so buyers outside the US should check which records the tool supports in their country.

Ruul's contractor management page describes onboarding contractors quickly, keeping invoices, payments and documents together, and running built-in KYC and AML checks. Its compliance evidence is identity and anti-money-laundering checks rather than a library of tax forms, so buyers should confirm which documents the tool collects for their contractors.

## Products checked and left out

Gusto, Multiplier and Papaya Global were on the candidate list. Their pages returned a refusal when we read them, so none of their claims is counted.

Constrafor's contract page covers contracts, and its invoice page covers contractor payments. Its onboarding claim appears only in a help answer, so onboarding is not confirmed. Its insurance-certificate dashboard is the compliance evidence we read.

Worksome's product page describes automated contracts and global payments for external workers. Its compliance claim appears on the pricing page as a paid add-on, not as part of the core product.

Worksuite describes managing contractor contracts and invoices. It checks insurance for independent contractors, but the policies are issued by a third party, so that is not counted as the tool's own compliance records.

## What the pricing looks like

This page does not compare prices. The profile for each product records the pricing basis the vendor page shows. Check that basis against the vendor page before comparing, and ask each vendor for a quote on the same number of contractors, countries and payment currencies.

## Testing before you pay

Pick three contractors with different countries or tax setups and run each through onboarding, one invoice and one payment. Check where the signed contract is stored, how the invoice is approved, and which compliance records the tool keeps after the payment. Ask in writing which documents the tool collects in each country you use.

The same test shows whether a tool fits the way your business pays. A company that pays a few contractors a month in one currency has different needs from a company that pays dozens in several countries. Ask each vendor to walk through the invoice path for a contractor in a country you use now, and to name the step where a payment can be held or reversed. Vendor pages describe the happy path. The approval and exception steps are where a contractor tool either saves time or adds a spreadsheet back into the process.
