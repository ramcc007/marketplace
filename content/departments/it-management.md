IT management covers the tools a technology team uses to take requests, keep track of what the company owns, and keep devices and data in working order. This department groups eight comparison pages. Each one compares eight products and links to the vendor pages we used.

## Requests, records and assets

Start here if the problem is knowing what IT is responsible for and who is asking for what.

[IT service management](/software/itsm) covers service desk products. They are compared on catalog and SLA packaging, certifications, trials and contract terms. [Configuration management database](/software/cmdb) looks at the records behind that work: configuration items, the relationships between them, and views that show what a change could affect.

[IT asset management](/software/it-asset-management) is the nearest neighbour. It compares tools on discovery, lifecycle tracking and license or contract tracking. Several products appear in more than one of these three categories, because some service desk suites include asset and configuration records.

## Devices and endpoints

These pages cover software that sits on, or talks to, the machines people use.

[Remote monitoring and management](/software/rmm) compares tools that put an agent on each device, then add scripting, patching and alerting. [Mobile device management](/software/mdm) is about enrollment, policy enforcement, and remote lock and wipe for phones, tablets and laptops. [Patch management](/software/patch-management) is narrower. It compares operating system and third-party patching, scheduling and compliance reports.

The overlap is real. An RMM tool often patches, and a mobile device tool may manage laptops. When two categories look alike, check each page's entry test.

## Data and software spend

[Backup](/software/backup) compares schedules, restore points, encryption, storage terms and how each product is bought.

[SaaS management](/software/saas-management) tracks the cloud apps a company pays for. The pages compare app discovery, license usage tracking and renewal management. The discovery source matters most, since a tool can only report on what its source can see.

## Prices and what to ask

Prices on these pages come from the vendors' own pages, checked at the source. Some vendors publish only part of their pricing. Freshservice, for example, lists Starter, Growth and Pro per agent a month and sells its Enterprise plan by quote. Where a vendor only quotes, the product profile says so rather than guessing a figure.

Before you shortlist, write down the number of agents or devices you manage and the one feature you cannot do without. Then compare the lowest plan that includes that feature, not the cheapest plan on the pricing page. Check contract terms too, since renewal notice and refund rules differ.
