Finance and Accounting is a wide department. A ledger, a card program and a tax filing tool share a finance team, but they solve different problems and are bought by different people. This page groups the live comparisons by the job they do, so you can find the right one quickly.

## Books, bills and invoices

[Accounting](/software/accounting) comes first because the other tools usually feed it. Its comparison looks at ledger and reconciliation access by plan, which is where plans tend to differ.

Money owed to suppliers and money owed by customers have their own tools. [AP automation](/software/ap-automation) covers invoice capture, approvals and vendor payments. [AR automation](/software/ar-automation) covers invoice delivery, payment matching and aging reports. [Invoicing](/software/invoicing) is the lighter option for a business that mainly needs to send a bill and see whether it was paid, and its comparison pays attention to free plan limits and e-invoicing rules.

## Spending and buying

Several categories deal with money going out. [Expense management](/software/expense-management) handles receipts, policies, approvals and reimbursement, and [Mileage tracking](/software/mileage-tracking) covers trip logging and per-mile rates. [Corporate cards](/software/corporate-cards) compares physical and virtual cards, spend limits, accounting sync and personal guarantees.

[Spend management](/software/spend-management) sits across those, adding purchase approvals and team budgets in front of the spending. [Procurement](/software/procurement) goes a step earlier, to purchase orders, approval controls and supplier onboarding. Vendors in these areas overlap, so a product may appear in more than one comparison. Read the category definition before assuming two tools do the same job.

## Billing, payments and tax

Companies that charge customers on a schedule or by consumption have a separate set of needs. [Subscription billing](/software/subscription-billing) covers recurring plans and merchant of record terms. [Usage-based billing](/software/usage-based-billing) covers event metering and tiered rating. [Dunning management](/software/dunning-management) deals with what happens after a card payment fails.

[Payment processing](/software/payment-processing) compares how businesses accept cards and wallets, receive payouts and screen for fraud. [Sales tax and VAT](/software/sales-tax) compares rate calculation, registration, filing and nexus monitoring.

## Planning, and how to read the prices

[Budgeting and forecasting](/software/budgeting-forecasting) compares tools for budget models, scenarios and variance reports.

Across the department, the prices in our comparisons come from vendor pages. Some vendors publish no prices at all and sell by quote only. Motus in mileage tracking is one example, and several budgeting and AR automation vendors work the same way. Those products are marked as quote only, so a missing number means the vendor did not publish one.

When you compare products, ask each vendor what the headline price excludes. Per-user fees, payment fees, card fees and plan limits on the ledger or on invoices can change the total a good deal. Also check which country the price applies to, because many of the figures are for US buyers.
