Security software covers very different jobs. Some tools sit on laptops, some read logs, some control who gets into an account, and some mainly produce evidence for an auditor. This department groups nine categories, each with a full comparison and product profiles.

## Protecting devices, data and mail

[Endpoint protection](/software/endpoint-protection) covers the agent on each laptop and server: malware detection, response after an attack, and a central console. Antivirus and endpoint detection and response can come in one license, so read what each plan includes before comparing prices.

[Email security](/software/email-security) filters phishing and malware before a message reaches a mailbox and gives administrators a quarantine to review. Ask each vendor how it spots a forged executive or brand.

[Encryption](/software/encryption) is really three kinds of product: key services, file and volume tools, and disk encryption for devices. A key service does nothing for a stolen laptop, so decide which job you need first.

## Seeing and fixing problems

[SIEM](/software/siem) collects logs from many sources, runs correlation rules and raises alerts an analyst can investigate. [Vulnerability management](/software/vulnerability-management) scans assets for known weaknesses, ranks the findings and tracks each fix. For both, ask how the tool shortens the list a small team must work through.

## People and access

[Multi-factor authentication](/software/multi-factor-authentication) adds a second proof of identity after the password. Compare the factors offered, the rules that decide when to ask, and the administrator's control over enrollment.

[Password manager](/software/password-manager) products keep shared logins in an encrypted vault. The cheapest plans cover sharing and basic control, while single sign-on can sit on a higher plan. Price the plan that has the features you need.

[Security awareness training](/software/security-awareness-training) pairs courses with simulated phishing emails and completion reports. Check that the reports show who finished what.

## Compliance and prices

[Compliance automation](/software/compliance-automation) maps your systems to frameworks such as SOC 2 and ISO 27001, collects evidence and gives auditors a place to work. The software does not certify anyone, so ask whether the audit is included, sold separately or left to you.

Prices on these pages come from vendor pages. Some vendors publish no amounts and show only a quote button, including Splunk for Enterprise Security, Mimecast for email security, and HashiCorp Vault and Azure Key Vault. The category pages mark those products as quote-only. Ask for a written quote early, and ask what is billed separately.
