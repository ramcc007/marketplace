HR software covers a wide span of work, from the day a candidate applies to the day a payslip is issued. B2BRated groups seventeen of its category pages under this department, and each one compares eight to eleven products against a short list of capabilities the vendors document themselves.

## Core records and pay

Most HR stacks start with a record of who works for the company. [HRIS](/software/hris) compares products on employee records, time-off tracking and HR reports. Pay sits next to it. [Payroll](/software/payroll) covers US services, looking at tax filing, direct deposit, add-on fees and contract terms. [Global payroll](/software/global-payroll) turns to providers that pay people across countries, with country coverage and local compliance as the main tests.

Companies that would rather not run these functions themselves have two other routes. [Employer of record](/software/employer-of-record) services employ workers on a company's behalf in another country, and the page weighs starting fees, deposits and classification risk. [PEO](/software/peo) pages cover professional employer organizations, where co-employment terms and IRS certification matter. [Benefits administration](/software/benefits-administration) looks at enrollment, carrier connections and ACA reporting.

## Hiring and onboarding

Recruiting has five pages. [Applicant tracking](/software/applicant-tracking) compares job posting, interview scorecards and record keeping. [Recruiting CRM](/software/recruiting-crm) is about talent pools and outreach, including the email and privacy rules that apply to it. [Background check](/software/background-check) covers screening providers, candidate consent and links to applicant tracking systems. [Pre-employment testing](/software/pre-employment-testing) examines anti-cheating controls and candidate scoring. Once an offer is accepted, [Employee onboarding](/software/employee-onboarding) covers checklists, e-signed paperwork and new hire access.

## Managing the workforce

Day-to-day people management is split by task. [Time tracking](/software/time-tracking) notes the plan where timesheet approval starts and whether a product offers payroll exports or monitoring. [Employee scheduling](/software/employee-scheduling) looks at shift swaps, SSO and the predictive scheduling laws. [Performance management](/software/performance-management) compares review cycles, goals and OKRs, and continuous feedback. [Employee engagement](/software/employee-engagement) concentrates on survey anonymity and reporting thresholds. [Corporate LMS](/software/learning-management-system) covers training platforms, with attention to assignment rules, reporting and security. [Workforce planning](/software/workforce-planning) is the forward-looking page, built around headcount plans, position control and budget scenarios.

## Prices and what to ask

Prices on these pages come from vendor pages, checked at the source. Some vendors publish a per-user monthly rate, and 15Five, for one, lists its Perform plan at $11 per user per month, billed annually. Others only quote. Accurate Background, in background checks, publishes no prices at all. Each category page marks which is which.

Before shortlisting, write down which of these functions you already have and which you want to replace, because many products span two or three pages here. Ask each vendor what sits in add-ons rather than the base plan, what the contract minimum is, and what happens to your data if you leave. For anything touching pay, hiring or monitoring, check the legal points the category page raises against the states or countries where your people work.
